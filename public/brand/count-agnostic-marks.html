The HyperVisuals Media Co. Identity — Round 03 Constraint: format count is variable, HP-3 → HP-10

Marks that survive
a changing count.

You were right to push back. A logo built on nine cells is a logo that breaks the first time you ship an HP-6. Here is what that actually costs, and three new marks built so the number can never break them.

What the correction costs: one mark, not the round.

Tesseract and Strata were never counting anything — Tesseract is a projection, Strata is six layers chosen for rhythm, not because six means six insights. Both carry forward untouched. Only Nine had the number wired into its meaning, and Nine is the one that dies.

The salvage is worth stating, though: Nine still works as a pattern system — endpapers, tile backgrounds, motion — where a shifting count is a feature. It just can't be the logo, because a logo has to be the same shape every time or it isn't a logo.

01

The Cut

New Variable — partition
HyperVisuals

One field — the whole source
Few cuts — placed by judgement, not by grid

The constant in your work was never the number of pieces. It's that somebody knew where to cut.

A grid divides evenly, which requires no skill. This divides unevenly — the cuts land where the material asked for them. That is the whole editorial act, and it is true of an HP-3 and an HP-10 alike.

Drag the cut count below from two pieces to six. The mark stays recognisably itself the entire way, because its identity lives in the kerf — the clean gap of separation — not in how many pieces the kerf produces. That is exactly the property you asked for.

It also gives you something the others don't: a legitimate reason for the mark to flex per format, while one canonical configuration stays locked as the logo.

The same mark at every format you ship

Two pieces through six, one canonical cut logic. Nothing about the mark's character changes — only how much of the field it has been asked to resolve.

02

Threshold

New Variable — depth
HyperVisuals

Solid planes — a floor and a wall
One opening — set off-centre, so it recedes

Not a frame. A room you are standing at the edge of, with the light coming from the far end.

Two solid trapezoids converge on an off-centre opening. Because the planes are filled rather than drawn in outline, it reads as mass and architecture instead of diagram — heavy, still, and built. Depth here isn't a metaphor about wisdom; it's literal perspective doing the work.

This is the answer to Tesseract's ownability problem. Same spatial idea, but a wireframe cube is a genre and a rendered corridor is a drawing. Nothing about it counts anything.

03

H-Gate

New Variable — letterform
HyperVisuals

Two piers, one lintel — the letter H
Lintel tapers — so the H becomes a gate

An H is already two posts and a beam. It is a doorway that happens to be a letter.

Set the crossbar as a plane that narrows toward the far pier and the letter stops being flat — you're looking through it. Pull depth to zero and it collapses back into an ordinary H, which is the test that the perspective is carrying real weight.

The monogram sheet you sent was all letterforms, so this is the direction closest to what you already like. It is also the most defensible: your own initial, cut a specific way, tied to no count and no format.

04

Tesseract

Carried forward Variable — dimension
HyperVisuals

Hyper — beyond, one dimension up
Unaffected — projects, never counts

Still the literal meaning of your name, and the count problem never touched it.

A hypercube is a cube's shadow from a dimension we can't stand in — which is what a two-hour episode becomes in your hands. Its weakness is unchanged too: this is a known move in tech branding, and the perspective offset is the only thing that turns a symbol back into a specific drawing.

05

Strata

Carried forward Variable — accumulation
HyperVisuals

Layers — chosen for rhythm, not for count
One seam — drifting left as it descends

Six bands because six looks right — nobody will ever read it as six of anything.

Weight increases downward, so the mark has gravity. The seam drifts as it descends, which is the part nobody else has: a straight cut is a barcode, a drifting one is a core sample. Set drift to zero and watch it die.

Recommendation — 01 The Cut

Keep the argument, drop the number

Nine was right about one thing and wrong about one thing. Right that the logo should encode what you actually do. Wrong about what that is — I mistook the output for the skill.

Your output is a variable number of cells. Your skill is knowing where the cuts go. The Cut encodes the skill, which is the thing that stays constant from HP-3 to HP-10 and into whatever format you invent next year. It keeps everything Nine was reaching for and takes on none of the fragility.
