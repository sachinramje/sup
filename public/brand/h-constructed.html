Constructed,

Four H's built from scratch on new geometry. No sliders, no variants — finished marks, at size, on the ground you picked.

What was wrong with everything I showed you before.

Every one of them was the same drawing: two rectangles and a bar, with a different edge treatment applied on top. Chamfer it, round it, flute it — the underlying shape never changed. That's styling, and styling is why they all felt interchangeable.

The sheet you picked doesn't work that way. Those glyphs are constructed — the silhouette is hexagonal, the counters are wedges, forms interlock and split. The letter is invented, not decorated. These four are built that way.

The recommendation — Interlock H

Two identical pieces, turned to face each other

The mark is one shape, drawn twice — the second is the first rotated a half turn. Together they make the letter; the diagonal between them is where they meet.

That's the whole construction. Nothing was added to make it interesting, and nothing can be removed without losing the H.

It also says the thing you actually sell: two halves nobody had put together, and the seam that proves it took a decision.

Across the brands

One H, every product. Volt is the signature; black is the workhorse.

Recommendation — 01 Interlock

The only one with a construction you can't argue with

Hexagon is the most immediately striking and I'd take it as a close second. Block is the safest. Seam is the boldest single gesture. But Interlock is the only one where the construction is discovered rather than imposed — the H was always two identical halves, and nobody had cut it there.

That matters for a mark meant to last, because there's no styling decision in it to go out of date. Change the weight, change the angle, redraw it in twenty years — it's still one shape placed twice.
