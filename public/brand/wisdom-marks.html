The HyperVisuals Media Co. Identity — Round 02 Brief: depth · structure · patience

Three marks that
slow down.

The first round leaned. Lean reads as speed, and speed is the opposite of what you sell. These three drop the shear entirely and build the mark out of the only three things wisdom actually looks like: dimension, order, and accumulation.

01

Tesseract

Variable — dimension
HyperVisuals

Hyper — beyond, one dimension up
Visual — the thing you can see of it

A hypercube is a cube's shadow from a dimension we can't stand in. It is the literal meaning of your name, drawn.

That's the whole argument. You are not called FastVisual. "Hyper" means the extra dimension — the thing that was always in the source but invisible until someone projected it down to where people live. That is precisely what a two-hour episode becomes in your hands.

It reads as intelligence without saying "brain," and as depth without saying "ocean." The eye has to resolve it — for a half second you see a flat diagram, then it pops into space and won't go back. That half second is patience, built into the mark.

The risk is real and I'll name it: tesseracts are a known move in tech branding. The defence is the perspective control below. Pushed off-centre, it stops being a symmetric diagram and becomes a specific view down a specific corridor — a drawing rather than a symbol.

02

Nine

Variable — order
HyperVisuals

Nine cells — your HP-9 format
One parameter — thin outline to solid

Your product is already a 3×3 grid of nine blocks. The logo should be the format.

Every HyperPod you ship is a nine-cell grid. If the mark is that grid, then every deliverable is also a logo impression — thousands of them, in the exact context where you want to be recognised. Nobody else can use this shape without advertising you. That is the compounding asset, and you already own it.

The construction is one idea, not nine: each cell is a square with a stroke, and the only thing that changes is the stroke weight. When the weight reaches half the cell, the square is solid — the outline has closed itself. So the progression from empty to full is a single continuous number, and the grid resolves as your eye travels it.

That progression is the wisdom argument, and it is the honest one: understanding does not arrive, it densifies. Run it top-to-bottom for sedimentation, or on the diagonal for a five-step build across the whole field.

03

Strata

Variable — accumulation
HyperVisuals

Six layers — thin at top, heavy at depth
One seam — the cut that drifts as it descends

Sediment. The oldest, densest material is at the bottom, and the only way to it is down.

Bands thicken as they descend, so the mark has a floor and a horizon — it has gravity, which none of the leaning options had. Read as rock strata it says deep time. Read as a stack of transcripts it says archive. Both are exactly the register you asked for.

The part that makes it yours is the seam. A vertical cut runs through every layer, and it drifts — each layer's gap sits slightly left of the one above. A straight cut would be a barcode. A drifting one is a core sample, a fault line, the path of something patient working its way down through material that keeps changing.

Straighten the drift to zero and you'll see the mark die instantly. That single offset is doing all the work, which is a good sign: it means the idea is load-bearing rather than decorative.

Recommendation — 02 Nine

Pick the one you're already printing

Tesseract is the cleverest and Strata is the best drawing, but Nine is the only one that gets stronger every time you ship. The other two are marks you'd have to spend money teaching people. Nine is a mark your product distributes for free, nine cells at a time, on every single visual that leaves the studio.

The same logic as the slash from round one: a rule beats a picture, because a rule extends into everything you make later and a picture just sits there. Nine is a rule about how your grid densifies.

Before you commit to one:

  1. Trademark search on the mark alone, in classes covering media production and software.
  2. Print all three at 12mm wide, in black on white, and look at them the next morning — not tonight.
  3. Test naked at 512px with no wordmark, then at 16px as a favicon. Nine survives; Strata loses its top band; Tesseract needs its heaviest weight.
  4. Whichever wins, lock its one number — cell gutter, inner scale, or seam drift — the way we locked the shear angle. That number becomes the system.
